Race detail: telling a certified result from an endorsement

Design pass for issue #354 · August 12, 2026 · three alternatives, one to ratify.

Every surface below is the real race-detail page, rendered from this repository's own committed data for Seattle City Council — District 5, with a certified results file attached. The tallies are invented for this pass: the 2026 primary certifies August 19 and no results file exists yet. Nothing else is a drawing — the meters, sources, names, and shares are the production components, so what these mockups compare is the actual DOM under three different treatments. Source lists are truncated to three rows each so the four surfaces stay comparable on one screen.

The complaint. On the race card, a certified result is one bounded section: a rule, a RESULT eyebrow, a Certified badge, then the tally. On this page the same information is spread across three places, and the vote share — the one that matters most — arrives as an unlabeled bar directly beneath the endorsement meter's own bar. Two bars, two percentages, and nothing saying that one counts organizations and the other counts voters.

What is fixed. The race card is frozen; this page adopts its vocabulary rather than inventing a second one. Results stay a state, not an option. Vote share and endorsement share never share a component. Every vote bar runs one full-width scale. The chip stays sky-on-navy, immediately after the name. Candidate order stays endorsement order.

The three alternatives

All three leave the top certified strip and the heading chip where they are. They differ in what they do with the vote-share row, which is where the confusion actually lands.

A · Certified band

The row stays exactly where it is and gains two things: the card's own RESULT eyebrow, and the tone band the certified strip at the top of the page already wears. The band is the recognition cue — a reader who has scrolled past the strip has already learned that this tint means certified fact — and the eyebrow names it outright.

B · The card's RESULT block, per candidate

The row becomes the race card's own anatomy at candidate scale: a rule, a RESULT eyebrow with the share opposite it, and the bar beneath. Each candidate section then reads as two labeled zones — what sources said above, what voters decided below — using the exact vocabulary the card taught on the guide page.

A trailing candidate

Every alternative preserves the muted trailing treatment (gray bar, lighter share) for a candidate who did not advance — the third section in each surface above. The chip is absent there, which is exactly the case where an unlabeled bar is most ambiguous today: no chip, no eyebrow, no word at all.

Narrow widths

A and B stack unchanged — both are single-column blocks already. C's three-column grid is the one that needs a stated fallback: the label column has to collapse above its row below roughly 30rem, or the meter is squeezed to nothing.

One thing none of the three fixes

Found while rendering the measure case above, and surfaced here rather than silently resolved: this page never states the whole tally. A candidate section exists only for a candidate some source endorsed, so a certified share renders only for endorsed choices. In the measure page above, "Yes" carries 60.2% and "No" — 39.8% of the same certified count — appears nowhere on the page at all. The same is true of the candidate race: Dimitri Georgakopoulos took 10.8% and has no section, because no source endorsed him.

The race card does not have this problem: its RESULT block lists every outcome, endorsed or not. So the card states a complete result and the page states a partial one — which is arguably a second, larger sense in which this page's results read as decoupled from the card's.

This is out of #354's own scope as filed, and none of A, B, or C addresses it: all three restyle a row that only exists where a section already does. It needs its own decision — either the page accepts that it annotates endorsed candidates only, or it gains a complete tally somewhere (the certified strip is the natural host). Worth settling at the same sitting, and filing separately.

Recommendation

B. The failure this ticket describes is a legibility failure — "you could be forgiven to not know what that represents" — and a label is the only thing that fixes legibility directly. A signals difference without naming it; C names both but pays for the pairing by narrowing the meter and reaching into structure #325 just settled. B says the word, in the vocabulary the card already taught, for the price of one line per candidate.

If B's vertical cost proves too high in live review, A is the fallback that keeps the label and gives up the bounded zone.

Nothing here is ratified. This page is the artifact for that conversation.
