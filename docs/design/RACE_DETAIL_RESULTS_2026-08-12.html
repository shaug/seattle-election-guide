Race detail: telling a certified result from an endorsement

Design pass for issue #354 · Ratified August 13, 2026 · amended into docs/RESULTS.md · implementation #370

Every surface below is the real race-detail page, rendered from this repository's own committed data for Seattle City Council — District 5, with a certified results file attached. The tallies are invented for this pass: the 2026 primary certifies August 19 and no results file exists yet. Nothing else is a drawing — the meters, sources, names, and shares are the production components, so what these mockups compare is the actual DOM under three different treatments. Source lists are truncated to three rows each so the four surfaces stay comparable on one screen.

The complaint. On the race card, a certified result is one bounded section: a rule, a RESULT eyebrow, a Certified badge, then the tally. On this page the same information is spread across three places, and the vote share — the one that matters most — arrives as an unlabeled bar directly beneath the endorsement meter's own bar. Two bars, two percentages, and nothing saying that one counts organizations and the other counts voters.

What is fixed. The race card is frozen; this page adopts its vocabulary rather than inventing a second one. Results stay a state, not an option. Vote share and endorsement share never share a component. Every vote bar runs one full-width scale. The chip stays sky-on-navy, immediately after the name. Candidate order stays endorsement order.

And one requirement settled after the first three mockups. The page has to state every result, not only the endorsed candidates' — a candidate nobody endorsed can win, and today that winner would not appear on the page at all. There is no point publishing results at all if the winner might be missing from them.

Read this as a record, not a menu. The three treatments below were drawn first, and then the completeness requirement changed the shape of the problem: the answer it landed on ("Showing every result," option 3) removes the per-candidate vote row entirely, and with it the question A, B, and C were three answers to. They are kept here because what was considered is part of the record, and because if the direction is reversed they are where it reverses to. The reasoning is under "Where this landed" at the end.

The three treatments superseded

All three leave the top certified strip and the heading chip where they are, and differ in what they do with the vote-share row. That row does not survive the direction this pass landed on, so none of these is live — they are the record of what was weighed, and the fallback if the direction reverses.

The band is the recognition cue — a reader who has scrolled past the strip has already learned that this tint means certified fact — and the eyebrow names it outright.

A trailing candidate

Every alternative preserves the muted trailing treatment (gray bar, lighter share) for a candidate who did not advance — the third section in each surface above. The chip is absent there, which is exactly the case where an unlabeled bar is most ambiguous today: no chip, no eyebrow, no word at all.

Narrow widths

A and B stack unchanged — both are single-column blocks already. C's three-column grid is the one that needs a stated fallback: the label column has to collapse above its row below roughly 30rem, or the meter is squeezed to nothing.

Showing every result

A candidate section exists only for a candidate some source endorsed (candidate_endorsement_groups, and docs/METER_V2.md decision 25: "no section, in either model"). A certified share therefore renders only for endorsed choices — and the choice nobody endorsed can be the one that won.

Below is that case, rendered from the same real page: Dimitri Georgakopoulos takes 40.1% and advances. No source endorsed him, so he has no section, and the page does not name the winner anywhere. The headline still reads "Leading choice: Nilu Jenks," which is correct — it is the recommendation, and outcomes never displace recommendations — but a reader has no way to learn who actually won. The measure page has the same hole: a rejected levy's winning "No" never appears.

Option 3 · The complete result under the race header the direction

The certified strip grows into the complete result, in the position it already holds: under the race header, above the "Counting all 40 sources" bar, above every candidate. It absorbs the strip's own date, authority, and ballot count — those become the block's provenance line — so nothing is stated twice, and the strip does not survive alongside it. The per-candidate rows go away entirely; each candidate section keeps only its chip.

Placement is the whole argument here, and there is exactly one seam it can use. Nothing can go between the headline and the first candidate section, because those two are one candidate's heading and body: the headline is the leading choice's heading (docs/DESIGN.md, "A name appears once per page"), and their meter opens the section below it. A block dropped there splits a name from its own meter. That leaves the page chrome above the headline, or the very bottom of the page — and the bottom is where option 2 already shows the winner going.

What this also does. It dissolves #354's original complaint rather than treating it: there is no longer an unlabeled bar beneath the endorsement meter, because the vote share no longer lives in the candidate stack at all. Each candidate section goes back to being purely what sources said.

The objection this raises, and its answer. The complete result now precedes the recommendation in reading order, and docs/RESULTS.md says outcomes are "rendered as context beneath the recommendation, never as the headline." Answered under "Where this landed" below: the result precedes the recommendation without replacing it, and the two regions are different in kind — one is the same for every reader, the other changes with the reader's own source selection. The amendment has to say so explicitly rather than let the placement imply it.

The cost that stands. Every candidate with both a section and a tally row is named twice on one page. The race card already does this and reads fine, but the card is one compact block and this page is long. Worth a live click-through before ratification.

Where this landed ratified · Aug 13, 2026

Option 3, on the maintainer's reasoning, ratified August 13, 2026 and amended into docs/RESULTS.md as "The race-detail page's certified result (2026-08-13 addendum, #354)". Implementation is #370. The reasoning, as it was given:

Once the election is over and the results are in, the results supersede endorsements on the race page. On the guide, a race card can show its recommendation and meter above its result, because a card is compact and nothing is buried. The race page is long: put the result after the endorsements and the one thing a reader most needs — who actually won — is the one thing they cannot see when the page loads.

And the reading-order objection does not survive contact with what the two regions actually are. The endorsement content does not need to be complete: it shows who was endorsed by the sources the reader cares about, and a lens changes it. The result is not source-specific and not endorsement-agnostic by accident — it is the same fact for every reader. The page already encodes exactly this: race.html.j2 renders the certified strip outside every lens-owned region because it is "a permanent fact the reader's source selection never changes," gated on race_results alone rather than on the personalization policy.

Which gives the page a real organizing principle rather than a placement preference:

  • Above the lens bar — facts no reader's selection can change: the race, the certified result, its provenance.
  • Below the lens bar — what the reader's chosen sources said, complete only with respect to that selection.

"Outcomes never displace recommendations" survives intact under that reading: the result precedes the recommendation, it does not replace it. The "Leading choice" headline keeps its position, its tone, and its meter, directly under the bar.

The four sub-decisions, answered

Settled with the maintainer, August 13, 2026:

  • The chip renders in both regions. "Advances" stays after each candidate's name in the endorsement stack as #287 ratified it, and also rides its row in the tally above. The boundary sorts what can change with a lens, not what may be mentioned twice; a reader scanning names below the bar should not have to scroll back up to learn who advanced.
  • The two orders stand. The tally runs in finish order, the sections in endorsement order. More than ordering differs: the tally can name candidates who have no section at all below it — that is the whole point of it, and the winner-with-no-endorsements case above is exactly that. The two regions answer different questions and are not expected to line up.
  • The counting window needs nothing. No results file exists, so neither the block nor today's strip renders and the page reads as it did before election day. Intermediate results would change that, and the site does not publish them (docs/RESULTS.md, "Posture: close the record"); if that is ever revisited, this is one of the surfaces it reopens.
  • Shared CSS moves to guide-race.css. The tally's head rules (.race-results-head, -eyebrow, -status) and its row, bar, name, and share rules live in guide.css today, which race pages do not ship. They move to guide-race.css, the sheet both pages already render (stylesheets.py). This is a correction rather than a new home: that module's own docstring already describes guide-race.css as "the narrower group only the guide and a race page render — the result block, the one meter, the reference bar," and .race-detail-result-chip already sits there for exactly this reason. Duplicating the rules into race.css instead would put two copies of one component's paint in two sheets. The card's own markup is untouched by the move.

Raised and routed elsewhere

  • The race page's lens bar is not position: sticky, unlike the guide's own identical strip. Filed as #369; not this pass's work. The two do not conflict — the result block is meant to scroll away, the strip is not.
  • A date bar on the race page, like the guide's. Not designed, not proposed here.
